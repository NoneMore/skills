# Local mathematical prose

Read this when editing sentences, paragraphs, theorem/proof narration, or text surrounding formulas. These are presentation diagnostics, not tests of mathematical validity.

## Match a sentence to its job

- **Introduce an object:** say what kind of object it is and why the reader needs its name, using only supplied information. Distinguish "let ... denote" (naming) from "define ... by" (stipulation); use "is given by" for an already-introduced object's displayed expression.
- **Lead into a display:** indicate whether the display records a definition, a previously stated relation, or a step the author has already described. Avoid empty leads such as "We have the following equation" when the manuscript supplies a more informative purpose.
- **Follow a display:** clarify a symbol, explain its stated role, or link to the next supplied step. Do not simply recite the formula in words, invent its interpretation, or increase the strength of the original assertion.
- **Narrate a proof:** give each paragraph a recognizable task and, where already present, an explicit transition to the next task. A brief roadmap can help a long proof, but derive it from the author's written structure rather than designing a new argument.
- **Present a result:** separate a theorem-like statement from surrounding motivation and reader guidance. Keep every mathematical qualifier and the strength of the claim exactly as authored.

## Repetition and terminology

- Prefer shorter syntax, clearer subjects, and better information order over synonym rotation.
- Keep established technical nouns and notation stable. A repeated sentence frame is useful when the mathematics is intentionally parallel; change it only when repetition obscures different expository roles.
- Reduce empty signposts ("it should be noted that", "we now proceed to") when the next sentence already makes the transition clear.
- Avoid attributing novelty, priority, strength, generality, or consequences that the source has not stated.

## Risky editorial moves

| Candidate change | Exposition-safe action |
| --- | --- |
| Replace repeated "we have" with a more informative lead | Use the author's explicitly supplied operation or relation; otherwise use a neutral introduction. |
| Change "then" to "hence", "therefore", or "equivalently" | Retain the claim-bearing connector unless the author confirms the intended force. |
| Shorten a theorem, hypothesis, or proof sentence | Retain every condition, qualifier, scope, and attribution; hold ambiguous compression for approval. |
| Add an intuitive explanation | Propose it separately if the author has not already stated that interpretation. |
| Rename a notation item or technical term | Keep the established form; ask first if a global rename is important to readability. |

## Local review

Read the revised paragraph together with its preceding and following paragraphs. Check that pronouns have clear referents; each display is introduced or integrated; first-use notation is oriented using existing definitions; transitions express a stated relation; and the text does not claim more than the original. Compare original and revised math spans, labels, citations, and draft markers literally where feasible. Do not treat the comparison as a mathematical proof check.
